GRAMMAR HUB / A2–B1

Mastering Gerunds

CORE CONCEPT

A gerund is a verb ending in -ing that works as a noun. It names an activity, idea, or experience rather than showing an action in time. Because gerunds behave like nouns, they can appear as the subject of a sentence, the object after a verb or preposition, or a complement that completes meaning. Compare “Swimming is relaxing” with “I enjoy swimming.” In both examples, swimming describes an activity, but its grammatical role changes. Gerunds can also take objects and adverbs: “Reading stories builds confidence.” Learning to spot the -ing form helps you understand sentence structure and speak with accuracy.

SEE IT IN A SENTENCE

01 Swimming is relaxing. gerund as subject
02 I enjoy swimming. gerund as object
03 Her favorite hobby is reading. gerund as complement

GRAMMAR ESSENTIALS

Mastering Infinitives

An infinitive is the base form of a verb preceded by 'to' (e.g., to learn, to speak). Infinitives can act as nouns, adjectives, or adverbs in a sentence. They often express purpose, intentions, future goals, or reasons for an action.

AS SUBJECTTo learn a new language takes patience.
AS OBJECTShe decided to study English today.
AS PURPOSE / ADVERBHe came to the library to read.

GRAMMAR ESSENTIALS / A2–B1

Mastering Quantifiers

Learn how to express amounts and quantities for countable and uncountable nouns.

Quantifiers tell us the amount or quantity of something. They change depending on whether nouns are countable (can count with numbers) or uncountable (cannot count individually).
SomePositive countable and uncountable nouns.some water · some apples
AnyNegative sentences or questions.any milk? · any books
ManyPlural countable nouns.many cars
MuchUncountable nouns.much time
A fewA small number of plural countable nouns.a few friends
A littleA small amount of uncountable nouns.a little money
